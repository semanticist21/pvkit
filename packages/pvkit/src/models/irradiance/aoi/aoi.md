# aoi — angle of incidence on a tilted surface

`aoiProjection` returns cos θ (the beam's projection onto the panel normal); `aoi` returns θ.

## Principle

`cos θ = cos β cos z + sin β sin z cos(γs − γ)` with tilt β, panel azimuth γ, solar zenith z
and solar azimuth γs (all degrees, azimuth from north clockwise). The projection is clipped
to [−1, 1] before `θ = acos(cos θ)` so rounding never yields NaN. θ > 90° means the sun is
behind the panel; callers floor `cos θ` at 0 when computing beam irradiance. No input
validation: the formula is defined for any angle.

## Reference

- **Spec:** J. A. Duffie, W. A. Beckman, *Solar Engineering of Thermal Processes*, 4th ed.,
  Wiley 2013, eq. 1.6.3, doi:10.1002/9781118671603.
- **Reference implementation:** `pvlib.irradiance.aoi` and `pvlib.irradiance.aoi_projection`
  @ pvlib 0.16.1.
- **Fixtures:** `aoi-fixtures.json` (138 cases: sun normal/behind/overhead, face-down,
  azimuth wrap, below-horizon, 100 random over the full angle ranges, 30 near-horizon zenith
  85–92°), generated by `scripts/fixtures/irradiance-aoi.py`.
- **Tolerance:** `1e-9` (degrees / unitless). Observed max error vs pvlib: 3.9e-14° (aoi),
  1.1e-16 (projection). Same closed form in float64; `acos` near ±1 amplifies 1-ULP
  projection differences to ~1e-6° only within ~1e-15 of the clip, which the fixtures avoid
  except at the exact-clip cases where both sides return 0/180.
